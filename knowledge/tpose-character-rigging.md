# Rigging T-posed AI characters by script: verified findings

Verified 2026-10-28 with Blender 5.3.0 Beta while building the cat, chimp, lioness and panda (`art/landmarks.py`, `art/animals.py` `'auto'` entries).

- **What the T-posed Tripo models look like**: one mesh, 1 unit tall, feet on z = 0, facing −Y, arms straight out along ±X with the hands as the outermost vertices, no rig, no racket, a 4096² base-colour JPEG. Loose pieces: shoes, laces, eye highlights, hats, ears, a pom-pom, straps and tools (4–25 pieces). Defects found: two 5-vertex cones on the chimp's cuffs, one zero-area face (lioness), 2–37 faces that "recalculate outside" flips (hair strands). Nothing else needed removing.
- **Joints that can be read off the mesh**: arm height from the hands' z; the shoulder as 3 cm inside the first 1 cm column (walking in from the fingertips) that reaches more than 0.28 below arm height (the body); wrist and fist a fixed hand length from the tips; the crotch as the lowest slice whose vertices reach the midline (restricted to the legs' own y, or a hanging tail triggers it); the ankle where the leg's slices stop being as deep as the shoe; the head top as the highest slice still half as deep as the head. Centroids pulled sideways by pouches and packs: take the trimmed midpoint of the slice and mirror-average left/right pairs.
- **Joints that could not be read reliably**: ears (hair, tufts and hats look like them) and tails (hair down the back, a hood, a sling bag): give them per character.
- **Heat weights on a T-pose** are clean under the arms (no shirt-drag rule needed) but still hand loose pieces to odd bones: a belt tool followed an arm. Copying the nearest big-piece vertex's weights onto every piece under 150 vertices fixes it; keep pieces that heat gave mostly to an ear or tail bone (a pom-pom tail).
- **Dropping the arms after skinning** with `apply_pose_as_rest` (arms aimed ~25–30° out of vertical, forearms ~12° forward, hands turned so their backs face out) gives a rest pose the shared arms-down clips can use; the sleeves fold acceptably on all four. A racket generated in the fist afterwards hangs beside the leg.
- **Racket face control**: `Anim.aim(normal=)` twists the hand about the shaft so the tool's face normal (stored per character in the hand socket's local frame) matches a target; with the targets read off the bunny's existing clips, the bunny's output is bit-for-bit the same and the other characters match it.
- **Short arms**: on these proportions (upper arm + forearm ≈ 0.1–0.15 of the height, head ≈ 0.35) the hand cannot get above the head or reach the other hand on the racket; poses are stylised, and tests must not assume the bunny's reach.
- **bash 3 (macOS)**: `"${extra[@]}"` on an empty array is an "unbound variable" under `set -u`; use `${extra[@]+"${extra[@]}"}`. This silently broke `art/build.sh` for every target except the landing island.
