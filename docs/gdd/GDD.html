01

The pitch

One sentence

Party games on the big screen. Your phone is the controller.

Mabar (short for main bareng, “playing together”) lets a host open a game on a laptop or TV, while friends scan a QR code and play on their phones.

This is a product pitch, not a validated business plan. Audience, market size, revenue model and retention evidence are not established yet.

02

Product vision

Make shared-screen play quick to start, easy to understand and fun to keep going. The host should spend game night playing, not setting up devices.

For the host

Choose a game, show the lobby QR, and let the group join without installs or accounts.

For the players

Scan, learn the controls in context, and play together using familiar or physical phone input.

For the group

Make room for people to join, leave, watch or switch games without breaking the gathering.

For the library

Offer compact, distinct games that share one quick-start platform and feel natural in a social room.

03

Design pillars

Start fast

One host link, one lobby QR, no downloads. Explain only what players need for the next action.

Make the screen a stage

Keep action, score and feedback readable from the couch. Give the whole room something to watch.

Include the group

Favor flexible teams, co-op, rotation or spectators over rules that leave people waiting.

Make games distinct

Share the platform foundation; let each game bring its own rhythm, skill and group dynamic.

06

World & identity

Visual language

Bright toy-diorama settings, rounded forms, warm light, saturated colors, friendly silhouettes and expressive characters.

Characters

All three games use the six animal characters (each phone plays its seat's animal; in Cooking they are empty-handed). Players do not pick their animal yet.

Places

The floating island is the hub. Tennis has its tropical island with a cheering crowd; Cooking has three dioramas (paddy field, beach, Pontianak riverside); Table Tennis still plays in the sports hall.

Open identity choices

Bunny mascot, Table Tennis venue and final product/repository naming remain open.

07

Platform rules

Shared screen

The host browser runs the game and shows the main action. Design for landscape screens and couch-distance readability.

Phones as controllers

Players join by QR or room code. Keep setup brief and controller prompts clear.

Keep the room together

Preserve connections between games where possible. Handle joins, departures and spectators gracefully.

Resilient play

Keyboard input supports host-side testing. Fallbacks should keep a game usable if an optional asset fails.

Current implementation uses static browser pages, Three.js and PeerJS/WebRTC, with no runtime framework or backend. See the README and Architecture document for technical contracts.

08

Cooking game design

A cooperative kitchen rush on Indonesian menus: Overcooked-style, built for a couch and four phones. The vocabulary is kept small (eleven crates, five station types, nine dishes) so that the layouts and the orders create the chaos, not the rulebook.

Design status (28 Oct 2026): implemented in js/games/cooking.js on the three dioramas from art/source/environments/cooking/. Everything marked in game can be played now with the keyboard chef or phones; numbers marked tuned on paper are first guesses that need a playtest with people (none has happened yet).
1–4 chefs, co-op4:00 per shift11 crates (8 families)5 station types + hatch + bin9 dishes, 6 spicy / extra variants3 kitchens

Shared cooking rules in game

Core loop

Orders appear top-left with a patience bar. Take an ingredient from a crate, prepare it (board or sink), cook it (pot or fryer) when the dish needs it, put the parts on a plate, carry the plate to the hatch. The team shares one score; a shift is four minutes, then stars.

One chef, one item

A chef carries one thing. A picks up, puts down and uses; X (held) works: chop, rinse, scrape a burnt pot; B dashes. Every counter tile holds one item, so staging and passing across a counter is part of the plan. Plates combine with what is on a counter in either direction.

Ingredients · 8 families, 11 crates

Rice, noodles, vegetables, tofu, seafood (fish, squid, shrimp, crab: one crate each, only on the map that cooks it), chili, citrus, peanut sauce. Crates never run out. Each has one preparation: chop at the board (vegetables, tofu, seafood, chili; citrus is “squeezed” there) or rinse at the sink (rice). Noodles and peanut sauce need none.

Stations · 5

Prep board (knife), rinse sink, cooker (a pot that boils up to three things together), fryer (one thing at a time), dish stack (clean plates; served plates come back after 9 s). Fixtures: the serving hatch and the bin. Plates, bowls and glasses are one “plate” in play; the container is drawn by the dish.

Timers

Chop 1.6 s, rinse 1.4 s of holding X. Boil 8 s (adding to a pot already cooking costs a little more), fry 6 s. Ready food waits 12 s in the pot and 10 s in the fryer, with a flashing badge and smoke in the last half, then burns: the station is blocked until someone scrapes it (X, 2 s). Chopped food never spoils. tuned on paper

Orders

Two orders open the shift, then one every 26 / 20 / 16 / 13 s for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 chefs, up to 3 / 4 / 5 / 5 waiting; the gap shrinks by a quarter over the shift. Three-part dishes are rare in the first 40 s, spicy and extra variants arrive after 90 s (about one in four), never three of a kind in a row. Patience is 50–84 s per dish, ×1.3 for one chef, ×1.1 for two. tuned on paper

Scoring and stars

20 points a dish plus a tip up to 12 for speed, +5 from the third dish in a row without a miss and +10 from the fifth. A missed order costs 10 and the combo. Wrong dish at the hatch: bounced, no penalty. Three stars at 26 × (6 + 3 per extra chef) points, two at 65 %, one at 35 %. tuned on paper

Choosing the kitchen

Once everyone in the waiting room has voted (or its clock runs out), the three kitchens appear as miniature dioramas on floating 3D cards, menu-style, with no text: left / right (stick, d-pad, ← → or a click) moves the highlight, A or the phone's button confirms, and after 30 s the highlighted one is taken. play.html?game=cooking&map=beach sets the practice kitchen.

Mistakes that teach

Every refusal says why on the TV (“Chop the tofu first”, “Only rice gets rinsed”, “Plate it before adding more”) and the phone's hint line says what to do with what you hold. Burnt food is loud (smoke, “Burnt!”) and costs time, not points: the penalty is the station you lose until it is scraped.

Solo and small teams

Fewer simultaneous orders and more patience (above). Lanes are at least one tile wide, chefs are round and push each other apart instead of locking, and dashing crosses a bridge in one burst.

AI teammates in game

The host sets the room to 2 or 4 chefs and the AI to Easy (rated 50 %) or Hard (90 %); the room card shows Players: 2/4 | Human: 1 | AI: 1 | Free: 2 | Difficulty: Easy. Phones take seats as they join; every other open seat gets an AI chef unless the host closes it. A phone that joins takes over an AI chef where it stands; one that leaves mid-shift is taken over by one. AI chefs play through the same controls and rules as a phone. Easy serves first come first served, thinks slower, now and then picks a worse job or a detour, and notices food about to burn late; Hard ranks orders by urgency against the work left, splits the work with the other AI chefs, dashes and rescues pots at once. With nobody joined, Watch AI cook runs AI-only shifts. For pacing and stars an AI chef counts as half a chef (Easy) or 0.85 (Hard). tuned in simulation

Animation

The six animals cook empty-handed (walk, run, side-step, an overhead chop while X is held, victory or defeat at the results); items ride in front of the chest. Pots steam while boiling, lids rattle when done, smoke rises before burning; the fryer basket dips and the oil bubbles; the sink splashes while rinsing; the board's knife chops; served dishes ring the hatch. The dioramas move by node name: palms sway, banners flutter, boats rock, gulls circle the beach.

Kitchens, ingredients & menus

Each kitchen is one AI-generated diorama (levelled, cleaned and gridded by art/kitchens.py) with the same station templates placed on a tile map (js/games/kitchens.js). The painted stations of the source models are removed and replaced, bridges are rebuilt on the grid, props on the walkways go; everything else stays. Vary the layout and the menu, not the vocabulary.

Why these rules rationale

Short recipes, long chains

Two- and three-part dishes keep a card readable from the couch, while the chain behind a part (take → chop → fry → plate) is where the teamwork lives. The pot that boils three things at once (Udang Saus Padang) is the one “advanced” move.

Counters everywhere

Overcooked's best moments are hand-offs. Every station row has free counters next to it and the bridges are the choke points, so passing a plate across a counter beats walking it round.

Burning costs a station, not the score

Losing 10 points for a burnt pot punishes the group; losing the pot for 2 s of scraping punishes the plan, which is what people laugh about. Missed orders are the only score penalty, and only −10.

Random but readable

Orders are drawn from three dishes per kitchen with weights, not from the whole book: a team learns a map in one shift. Variants come late and are marked in red, so a “Pedas” never surprises a chef mid-plate.

Four minutes

Three felt short for a four-chef team to find a rhythm in the prototype; five drags for a party. Four minutes with the order gap tightening keeps the last minute frantic.

Open: a second shift

Not built: a second shift on the same map with a higher par, washing-up (dirty plates), fire, and a fourth map. Decide after a playtest, not before.

Playtest questions

  • Does a 2-chef team reach two stars on the Paddy Field in its second shift? (par = 234 points)
  • Is 12 s of grace in the pot enough with four chefs and two cookers on the Beach?
  • Do people use the counters to pass, or walk everything round?
  • Is “hold X” discoverable on the phone without the hint line?
  • Do Easy AI teammates feel helpful rather than in the way, and does Hard feel like a strong friend, not a bot doing everything?
09

Growing the library

A new Mabar game should give the group a distinct social experience and fit the quick-start shared-screen platform.

Before building

Write a one-line hook, player count, core loop, controls, round length, win condition, join/leave behavior and one playtest question.

Before adding it

New players should understand the goal quickly; the TV should communicate progress; the main interaction should be fun before progression or cosmetics.

Needs real playtesting

Character selection, Table Tennis venue, sound/music, popularity voting, a second cooking shift (washing-up, fire), future game choices and final repository name.

Market research, audience validation, retention, monetization and business model are not established. Avoid presenting them as facts.
