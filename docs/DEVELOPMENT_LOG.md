## 2026-10-28 (night) — The racket meets the ball: dives, lunges, smashes, real ball physics

Owner request, with a Switch Sports tennis clip (`art/reference/gameplay/tennis/`, YouTube short `TQorjBl0vdU`): the characters dash for the ball, which looks real and fun, so give Mabar that; make the ball behave correctly when it is hit high, smashed or straight; and only return the ball when it is near the racket (a swing returned it from half way down the court).

- **Reference** ([knowledge](../knowledge/tennis-reference-footage.md)): watched frame by frame with `/skill-watch`. The "dash" is a full-length dive: 5–8 frames in the air (170–270 ms) covering 2–4 body heights with the racket out ahead of the head, a belly landing that slides, 0.2–0.45 s on the court, a quick get-up; it returns a weak pop-up, and a late one misses.
- **Cause of the bad returns**: a swing counted up to 0.40 s (more for remote players) before the ball reached its contact point, and the return was launched from the contact point at once, so the ball jumped from wherever it was.
- **Strokes** (`js/games/racket.js`): a swing in time (−0.10..+0.30 s) plans a stroke and is held until the ball gets to the racket; the player is placed from where each animal's racket head is on the stroke's contact frame (measured from the clips at runtime, `MabarChars.reachOf`), and the clip is timed so that frame lands on the ball. A forehand (with the arm lifted or dropped for a ball off its height), a lunge for a ball a step too far, a dive (Tennis) that flies up to 1.6 m and leaves the diver down for 0.85 s, a smash with a jump for a ball above the head. The hit happens only if the body got there; otherwise "So close!". The AI swings through the same path. A flash marks each hit, dust a dive's landing; the camera tilts up for lobs.
- **Ball flight** rewritten in meters, closed form ([knowledge](../knowledge/ball-flight-closed-form.md)): gravity, linear drag, spin, bounces that keep ~¾ of the speed, net crossing from the real arc, the stands' rails as walls. Lob (5.5–8 m high, slow, bounces high), drive (fast and low, flat when swung down), smash (fast, down), dive pop-up (high, slow, short), serve from the toss over the strings. The receiver's contact point is at their measured forehand or overhead height: on the way down, on the rise when that would be too deep, overhead before the bounce for a lob they can get under.
- **Clips** (`art/clips.py`, 16 now): `smash`, `lungeL/R`, `diveL/R`; contact frames in `CONTACT` (and `js/character.js`); lying poses grounded on the evaluated mesh. **Characters**: timed `play()` / `swing()`, `toolHead()`, `reachOf()`, `raise()`.
- **Tuning**: Tennis pace 1.55 − 0.6·power s line to line (was 2.1 − 0.9: under real gravity that pace made every drive a 3–4 m moonball), serve 1.7 − 0.5·power, deepest 1.28, `dive` 1.6 m. Table Tennis: animals 1.8 (was 1.55, their paddle was below the table), faster pace (0.62 − 0.27·power), lower bounce, a higher camera, lunges but no dives.
- **Verified**: `npm run smoke` (16 clips, where each stroke's racket reaches, the dive pose, both games with every return struck within 0.45 m of the racket head, swing direction with the return held until contact, forced smash / dive / early whiff / lob vs drive / bounce); AI matches logged the racket head within 0.01–0.26 m of the ball on hits (worst seen 0.37 m); slow-motion filmstrips of the dive, lunge, smash, lob, drive, serve and a Table Tennis forehand in headless Chrome; Blender previews of the new clips on the bunny, cat and chimp. **Not verified**: people playing (whether 0.30 s is early enough for real phones, how often the AI dives, the faster Table Tennis), real phones (the swing direction readings are still unconfirmed), slower GPUs.

## Open issues

- No automated tests, linting or CI; regressions are caught only by manual play.
- `npm run assets` (all targets) would regenerate the procedural `.glb` files with Blender 5.3; they were exported to a temp dir to check the scripts run, but not regenerated or compared.
- Animation limits of the source models: the short arms barely reach above the head, so the trophy pose and overhead serve are stylized (more so on the four T-posed animals, whose arms are shorter still and whose free hand hovers near the racket instead of gripping it in the ready position); the remodelled left arms of the bunny and pup still show small patched seams close up, and the bunny's left sleeve creases when that arm crosses the body.
- Phone motion: swing detection, `dx`/`dy` and the tilt are untested on real devices (the owner's phone reported no sensor in Tennis; the cause is unknown until the new readout is read on it); the sign of iOS's acceleration values is handled by a guess from the upright grip; the thresholds are guesses.
- Racket feel: `crossTime`, `deepest`, the shot depth/peak formulas and the AI's aim are first guesses tuned against the AI demo, not against people.
- AI chefs: tuned in simulation only. With an idle human, one Easy AI chef serves ~2 dishes a shift on the Beach (long walks across bridges); the AI weights for pace and par (0.5 / 0.85) make three stars easy for a team with Easy AI on the smaller kitchens. A human holding an item counts as covering that part of an order, so an AI chef waits for them.
- Phone controller: the portrait layout, the follow-the-thumb stick and auto-reconnect are checked in emulation only, not on iPhone or Android hardware.
